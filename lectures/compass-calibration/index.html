Part 1

The Earth's magnetic field

The field at a location is a 3D vector

Heading from a magnetometer

Roll $\phi$ and pitch $\theta$ come from the IMU. Rotate the reading $m$ (body frame) to a level frame:

$$m_\ell = R_y(\theta)\,R_x(\phi)\; m$$

The horizontal part gives the magnetic heading; add the declination:

$$\begin{aligned}\psi_{mag} &= \operatorname{atan2}(-m_{\ell,y},\; m_{\ell,x})\\ \psi &= \psi_{mag} + D\end{aligned}$$

Only one angle

The magnetometer adds yaw only; roll and pitch are already known from gravity.

Part 2

What the sensor measures

An ideal sensor on a rotating aircraft

The sensor measures in the body frame: $m = R^\top e$ changes as the aircraft turns. Rotated back with the IMU attitude, $R\,m = e$: the yellow arrow never moves.

Hard iron: a magnet the aircraft carries

Parts with their own permanent magnetism:

  • magnets in the motor, servos, buzzer
  • magnetised steel screws, standoffs
  • the sensor's own zero offset

Their field at the compass is the same vector $h$ in the body frame, wherever the aircraft points:

$$m = R^\top e + h$$

Typical size: tens to hundreds of mG — comparable to the horizontal field ($H \approx 190$ mG in Warsaw).

Look-alike: current in power wires — the same shift, but it changes with throttle (COMPASS_MOT).

Hard iron while the aircraft rotates

The red arrow $h$ is fixed to the aircraft (drawn at the compass) and turns with it; moved to the tip of $e$ it shows the reading $e + R\,h$, which wobbles around $e$: its length and direction change with the attitude. The tip moves on a sphere of radius $|h|$ around the tip of $e$.

Soft iron: steel on the aircraft, magnetised by the Earth

Earth's field: fixed in the world, points to magnetic north.
Steel spar (in the wing) and compass: fixed in the aircraft, turn with it.
The field magnetises the spar along its length; the spar adds that to what the compass reads.

Only the part of the field along the spar is amplified:

$$m = b + k\,(u\cdot b)\,u = \underbrace{(I + k\,u u^\top)}_{W}\, b$$

In the aircraft frame the tip of $m$ follows an ellipse, stretched along the spar.

Spar along the fuselage: the same, turned by 90° (N/S longer, E/W unchanged, heading error of opposite sign).

Real effect: a few % ($k$ exaggerated here).

Soft iron while the aircraft rotates

The induced part (purple) follows the spar: largest when the spar points along the field, zero across it. Unlike hard iron it scales with the field. (Exaggerated here: 80 % instead of a few %.)

All readings in the sensor frame: a sphere

Attitude $R_i$ (body → NED), Earth field $e$:

$$b_i = R_i^\top e, \qquad \|b_i\| = F$$

Every reading lies on a sphere of radius $F$ centred at $0$.

Press collect: one dot per reading while the vehicle is turned by hand.

Hard iron: the sphere moves

Every reading gets the same $h$ added:

$$m_i = b_i + h$$

The sphere keeps its radius, its centre moves to $h$.

A 250 mG offset is larger than the whole horizontal field: the uncorrected heading is useless.

Soft iron and gain: the sphere stretches

Every reading is multiplied by the same $W$:

$$m_i = W b_i, \qquad b_i = R_i^\top e$$

One spar (slide 10): $W = I + k\,uu^\top$. Several pieces of steel, unequal axis gains: more terms of the same form, so $W$ stays symmetric:

$$W = I + \textstyle\sum_j k_j\, u_j u_j^\top = Q\,\Lambda\,Q^\top$$

The sphere becomes an ellipsoid: axes along the columns of $Q$, semi-axes $\lambda_i F$ (purple: fitted).

A rotation of the sensor is not of this form — see part 4.

The model and the correction ArduPilot stores

$$m = W\,b + h + n$$

$m$: reading · $b = R^\top e$: Earth field in the body frame · $W$: soft iron and gains · $h$: hard iron (fixed in the body) · $n$: sensor noise

$$\hat b = s\, M\,(m + o), \qquad M \propto W^{-1},\; o = -h$$

$\hat b$: the corrected reading, an estimate of $b$

Part 3

Fitting the ellipsoid

Calibration = least squares on a sphere

Find $o$, $M$ (symmetric) and $r$ so that every corrected sample has the same length:

$$\min_{o,\,M,\,r}\; \sum_i \big(r - \|M(m_i + o)\|\big)^2$$

No heading, no position and no field direction are needed: any field gives a sphere.

fitness = RMS residual (mG).

CompassCalibrator

  1. collect 300 samples, each far enough from the others
  2. sphere fit ($o$, $r$), Levenberg–Marquardt
  3. drop samples that are now too close; collect again
  4. ellipsoid fit ($o$, diag, off-diag)
  5. accept if: $150 < r < 950$ mG, $|o_k| < 1800$, $0.2 < M_{kk} < 5$, $|M_{kl}| < 1$, fitness $\le$ COMPASS_CAL_FIT (16)
  6. scale to the local field (needs a position)
  7. check the orientation (uses the IMU)
  8. save; reboot

Try it: hard iron + soft iron + noise

Why turn it in every direction?

A flat spin on a table: $b_z$ is the same for every sample — the readings lie on one circle.

The $z$ offset, the $z$ gain and the radius cannot be separated: the fit is ill-conditioned (compare the one-face checkerboard in DLT, lecture 2).

ArduPilot tracks which of 80 cells of a sphere have samples and only accepts a new sample far from the others.

The scale needs a position

After the fit (fix_radius):

$$s = \texttt{COMPASS\_SCALE} = \frac{F_{\text{WMM}}(\text{lat}, \text{lon})}{r}$$

  • corrects the sensor's gain: a property of the chip, not of the place
  • rejected if $s \notin [0.67,\ 1.5]$: "bad radius"
  • position from GPS or from an EKF origin set by a companion

Part 4

Orientation relative to the IMU

Who sets the rotation?

  • an internal compass is rotated together with the IMU — it stays aligned automatically
  • an external compass (GPS mast) has its own COMPASS_ORIENT: one of ~40 standard rotations, or a custom one
  • the list holds fixed rotations only (mostly multiples of 45°/90°): a tilt of a few degrees cannot be expressed

Detecting a wrong orientation

Each sample is stored with the IMU attitude $R_i$. For a candidate rotation $C$:

$$\hat e_i = R_i\, C\, m_i$$

Right $C$: every $\hat e_i$ equals the Earth field $e$ — a tight cluster. Wrong $C$: a smear.

Pick the smallest variance; accept if the runner-up's is > 2× larger. COMPASS_AUTO_ROT = 2 fixes external compasses.

A small tilt is invisible to calibration

Tilt the sensor by a small rotation $P$. Seen from the centre $c$ of the cloud, every sample is rotated:

$$m_i' - c' = P\,(m_i - c)$$

  • fitness, radius, scale, pass/fail: unchanged
  • $M' = P M P^\top$: still symmetric, fits as well
  • offsets change by only $\approx \delta\,\|h\|$ (2°, 150 mG: 5 mG)
  • the corrected output is still rotated by $P$

Heading error of a level vehicle, tilt $\delta$ about the roll axis:

$$\Delta\psi \approx \delta \tan I \cos\psi$$

Leftover errors → heading error

hard iron: one cycle per turn, $\le \arcsin(e/H)$
soft iron: two cycles per turn
tilt: one cycle, $\times \tan I$; declination: constant
Part 5

After calibration

Calibrated in Opole, flying in Tallinn

No recalibration because of the place: $o$, $M$, $s$ describe the vehicle and the sensor.

Updated at run time from the position:

  • declination: at the first position after every boot
  • expected Earth field (WMM): in the EKF, at 1 Hz
  • field strength and inclination: learned in flight

Recalibrate after hardware changes, transport near magnets, a crash, or failed checks (ARMING_MAGTHRESH).

But the same leftover error costs more where $H$ is small:

Declination

$$\psi_{\text{true}} = \psi_{\text{mag}} + D$$

  • COMPASS_AUTODEC = 1: $D$ is looked up in the built-in table at the first position after each boot; set, not saved
  • EKF3 with a valid COMPASS_SCALE: table value at the current position, 1 Hz
  • no position at all: the stored COMPASS_DEC — often 0

GPS-denied flight

A declination error $\Delta D$ rotates every heading: navigating by heading drifts sideways by $\sin \Delta D$ of the distance. Tallinn without declination: $\sin 9.6° \approx 17\,\%$.

Set the EKF origin (SET_GPS_GLOBAL_ORIGIN) before take-off, or save COMPASS_DEC.

Part 6

The compass inside the EKF

Two ways to use the compass

Heading fusion

Tilt-compensate the reading and fuse one angle:

$$\psi_{meas} = \operatorname{atan2}(-m_{\ell,y},\, m_{\ell,x}) + D$$

  • no magnetic states; nothing is learned
  • a leftover offset goes straight into the heading
  • only the angle is checked: a wrong field strength or inclination passes unnoticed
  • robust when the surroundings change (near steel, on the ground)

3-axis fusion

Fuse all three components; six extra states, Earth field $B$ (NED) and body bias $d$:

$$m = R(q)^\top B + d$$

  • learns leftover offsets and the local field in flight
  • all three components are checked: inconsistent readings are rejected
  • needs attitude changes to separate $B$ from $d$
  • learns a disturbance (steel, ground) as "truth"

One 3-axis update

  1. predict $\hat m = R(q)^\top B + d$; innovation $\nu = \hat m - m$
  2. noise $R = \sigma_m^2 + (0.005\,\omega)^2$, $\;\sigma_m$ = EK3_MAG_M_NSE = 50 mG; grows with the turn rate $\omega$ (timing errors)
  3. innovation variance $S = H P H^\top + R$
  4. gate: $\nu^2 / (g^2 S) < 1$, $\;g$ = EK3_MAG_I_GATE/100 = 3; any axis fails → the whole sample is rejected
  5. fuse $x$, then $y$, then $z$ (scalar updates)
  6. clamp $B$ to WMM ± EK3_MAG_EF_LIM (50 mG)

$$H = \Big[\tfrac{\partial (R^\top B)}{\partial q}\ \Big|\ 0\ \Big|\ R^\top\ \Big|\ I_3\Big]$$

Why 3-axis fusion needs motion

A Copter flight (EK3_MAG_CAL = 3)

  • the ground is magnetically dirty (rebar, cars, the launch box): learning waits until the vehicle is clear of it
  • climbing with a growing yaw innovation and little rotation → an interim reset ("climbing out of an anomaly")
  • planes (EK3_MAG_CAL = 0) use a GPS-velocity yaw reset instead

Health, failsafe, logs

  • healthy sample: all three test ratios < 1
  • Copter EKF failsafe (FS_EKF_THRESH = 0.8): two of {compass, velocity, position} above the threshold. With heading fusion only the angle is tested — a weaker failsafe.
  • switching compass: rejected for 10 s, in flight, > 30 s after start, more than one compass
  • planes with GPS: last compass times out → compasses failed for this flight; yaw from GPS / GSF
